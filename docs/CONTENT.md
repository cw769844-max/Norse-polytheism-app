# Content guidelines

Hearth & Well serves people who care about getting the tradition right. These rules
apply to every piece of lore, tide description and ritual text in the app.

## Source tiers

Every claim is labelled with one of four tiers:

| Tier | Meaning | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 📜 Attested | Stated in a primary source (Eddas, sagas, skaldic verse, runic inscriptions, contemporary chronicles) | A citation to the work and chapter or stanza |
| 🔬 Scholarly | Academic reconstruction, archaeology, or documented later custom | A citation to the scholarship or source of the custom |
| 🔥 Community | Common modern Heathen practice | A short description of who practices it |
| 🌙 Personal gnosis (UPG) | One person's spiritual experience | Must be clearly marked; never presented as tradition |

Never blur the tiers. For example, if a festival's name comes from a saga but the
festival itself is modern, say so (see Freyfaxi).

## Citations

- Name the work and its location, e.g. `Ynglinga saga, ch. 8 (Heimskringla)`.
  Chapter numbers differ between editions; note the edition when it matters.
- Quote only from public-domain translations, or from our own translations.
- **Review status:** the citations shipped so far were written from general
  knowledge of the sources and **must be checked against the texts by a scholar of
  Old Norse before public release.**

## Primary texts

- Poems live in `src/content/poems/<id>.json` and are generated by
  `scripts/import_bellows.py`; don't edit the JSON by hand. To add a poem, add it
  to `POEMS` in the script and in `src/content/poems/index.ts`.
- The current translation is Henry Adams Bellows (1923), which is in the public
  domain. Stanza numbers follow his edition, which differs slightly from others
  (his Hávamál has 165 stanzas), so always cite them as Bellows' numbers.
- Bellows' notes are shown as 🔬 Scholarly, with a caution that some of their
  views are dated.
- The Old Norse is the text Bellows translated: Hildebrand and Gering's 1904
  edition (public domain), transcribed in `content-sources/hildebrand-gering-1904/`
  and proofread against the page images. Its README explains the file format,
  the editorial marks, and the one place Bellows reorders stanzas (Hávamál
  88/89). Correct the transcription there, never in the generated JSON.
- To add another poem's Old Norse, transcribe it from the same edition so the
  stanza numbers keep matching Bellows.

## Runes

- Younger Futhark is the primary reference; Elder Futhark is included as its
  historical predecessor and for modern practice.
- Rune meanings come first from the Norwegian and Icelandic rune poems
  (📜 Attested), in a public-domain edition such as Bruce Dickins, *Runic and
  Heroic Poems of the Old Teutonic Peoples* (1915), with the Old Norse alongside.
- Elder Futhark rune names (*fehu*, *ūruz*, …) are reconstructions (🔬 Scholarly).
- Divinatory and esoteric meanings are modern (🔥 Community or 🌙 UPG) and are
  always labelled as such.
- Don't say the Eddas were written in runes: they survive in Latin script.

## Stanza of the day

The curated list is in `src/features/daily/dailyStanza.ts`, and each entry needs
a reflection prompt in the locale file. The prompts are our own words: questions
for reflection, not claims about the tradition. Leave out stanzas that are
fragmentary or that disparage women.

## Values

The app is for everyone called to the gods, ancestors and wights of the North,
whatever their ancestry. Content must not use the tradition to exclude anyone on
grounds of race, ethnicity, gender or sexuality.

## Translation

All text lives in `src/i18n/locales/<lang>.json`. Proper names (works, Old Norse
terms) stay in their original form. Translations should be made or reviewed by
fluent speakers.
