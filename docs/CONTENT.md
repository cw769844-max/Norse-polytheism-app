# Content guidelines

Hearth & Well serves people who care about getting the tradition right. These rules
apply to every piece of lore, tide description and ritual text in the app.

## Source tiers

Every claim is labelled with one of four tiers:

| Tier | Meaning | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 📜 Attested | Stated in a primary source (Eddas, sagas, skaldic verse, runic inscriptions, contemporary chronicles) | A citation to the work and chapter or stanza |
| 🔬 Scholarly | Academic reconstruction, archaeology, or documented later custom | A citation to the scholarship or source of the custom |
| 🔥 Community | Common modern Heathen practice | A short description of who practices it |
| 🌙 Personal gnosis (UPG) | One person's spiritual experience | Must be clearly marked; never presented as tradition |

Never blur the tiers. For example, if a festival's name comes from a saga but the
festival itself is modern, say so (see Freyfaxi).

## Citations

- Name the work and its location, e.g. `Ynglinga saga, ch. 8 (Heimskringla)`.
  Chapter numbers differ between editions; note the edition when it matters.
- Quote only from public-domain translations, or from our own translations.
- **Review status:** the citations shipped so far were written from general
  knowledge of the sources and **must be checked against the texts by a scholar of
  Old Norse before public release.**

## Values

The app is for everyone called to the gods, ancestors and wights of the North,
whatever their ancestry. Content must not use the tradition to exclude anyone on
grounds of race, ethnicity, gender or sexuality.

## Translation

All text lives in `src/i18n/locales/<lang>.json`. Proper names (works, Old Norse
terms) stay in their original form. Translations should be made or reviewed by
fluent speakers.
