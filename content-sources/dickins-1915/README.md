# Rune poems: Dickins (1915)

Transcribed from Bruce Dickins, *Runic and Heroic Poems of the Old Teutonic
Peoples* (Cambridge University Press, 1915), pages 24–33: the Norwegian and
Icelandic rune poems, in Old Norse with Dickins' English translation. Public
domain (published 1915). Scan: the Wikisource index
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index:Runic_and_heroic_poems_of_the_old_Teutonic_peoples.djvu

## How it was made

Each page was read from the page images and checked word by word against three
independent OCR scans of the book on the Internet Archive (Toronto, Cornell and
a second scan). Every word was found in the OCR text, allowing for the way OCR
renders ð, ø, ǫ and þ in this typeface. Dickins' footnotes are not transcribed.

## The poems (from Dickins' introduction)

- **Norwegian:** first printed by Ole Worm in 1636 from a law manuscript that
  perished in the Copenhagen fire of 1728; Dickins ascribes it to a Norwegian
  author of the end of the 13th century, and prints it in normalized Old
  Norwegian spelling.
- **Icelandic:** thought to date from the 15th century; edited from four
  Arnamagnæan manuscripts. Each stanza ends with a Latin gloss of the rune's name
  and a word for "king" that alliterates with it.

Both poems describe only the sixteen runes of the Younger Futhark.

## File format

```
<stanza number>
<line>
><indented line>
~<Latin gloss>|<word for "king">     (Icelandic only)
```

`[ ]` in the Icelandic text marks a word supplied by the editor.
